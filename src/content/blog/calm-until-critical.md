## The idea

A home maintenance app is an anxiety machine if you let it be. There is always something you could be doing to your house. HomeRun's first design principle is that the default state of the UI should be calm, and urgency signals should be rare enough to still mean something.

## Points to make

- Red is a budget, not a color. Spend it on genuinely critical overdue items and nothing else — not on decoration, not on structure, not on "due soon."
- The gradient in practice: upcoming is neutral gray, due today is mild amber, a missed routine task gets a muted "Past due" label, and only a missed critical task goes full red. The task slipped; the house is fine.
- Most tools do the opposite because urgency drives short-term engagement. It also drives people to uninstall, and a maintenance app you uninstalled is worth nothing.
- The related principle: completion is the core interaction, so optimize that path relentlessly. Quick-complete from the dashboard, nothing required beyond the tap, optimistic update, undo toast, never make the user compute the next due date.

## Wider point

This generalizes past home maintenance to anything notification-driven — alerting, CI, monitoring, task managers. The credibility of your urgent state is a shared resource that every non-urgent red badge spends down. Could pull the on-call pager analogy in here.
